# --interactive--

There are two common forms of abbreviations: acronyms and initialisms.

- Acronyms are formed from the initial letters of a phrase and pronounced together as a single word, such as NASA or FEMA.

- Initialisms are also formed from initial letters, but each letter is pronounced individually, such as FBI or NCIS.

In HTML, you do not need separate tags for these. The abbreviation element, `<abbr>`, is used to display both.

To keep your content clear and accessible, you should always denote the full, unabbreviated form of a term on at least its first use in case readers are unfamiliar with it.

The `title` attribute is the easiest way to attach that full definition directly to the element. Setting the unabbreviated phrase as the `title` value displays a helpful tooltip pop-up when a user hovers over the `<abbr>` content:

:::interactive_editor

```html
<p><abbr title="HyperText Markup Language">HTML</abbr> (HyperText Markup Language) is the foundation of the web.</p>
```

:::

Usually, the style of the abbreviation element will change when you add this attribute. The specific style will depend on the browser. Some browsers may display a dotted underline, while others may convert the contents to small caps. When the user hovers over the abbreviation, the full form is displayed as a tooltip.

By pairing a clear text expansion on first use with the `<abbr>` element and `title` attribute, your abbreviations remain readable for all users and semantically meaningful for browsers and assistive technologies.
